# Thinking Detectives Character Cast Pool

## Purpose

This document preserves possible recurring characters and comic concepts for future Thinking Detectives cases. It is a world-building resource, not a required cast list and not a Case 02 plan.

The characters were created by Amira and reflect the kind of personality-driven humor she currently enjoys. They may live and behave like people in the Thinking Detectives world; their species does not require an underwater setting.

## Usage rules

- Remy remains the main recurring character and thinking sidekick.
- Do not force every character into the same case.
- Select a supporting character only when the character improves the mystery, adventure, emotional rhythm, or reasoning situation.
- Let humor arise from personality and consequences rather than disconnected gags.
- Preserve each character's competence and agency even when a comic flaw creates trouble.
- A catchphrase should remain funny through timing and variation; do not repeat it mechanically.
- Keep conflict playful and age-appropriate. In particular, Frank and Cammy's dynamic should read as absurd, Muppet-like comic tension rather than sustained bullying or helplessness.
- New characters may be created when the story needs a role this pool does not provide.

## Optional characters

### Sunburned Calamari

A tanned squid who is carefree, playful, and quite smart. His friends call him **Calamari**.

- **Comic engine:** His confidence and appetite for risk lead him to underestimate what could happen next.
- **Catchphrase:** “What could go wrong?”
- **Recurring payoff:** Something then goes wrong.
- **Potential story value:** Can propel the group into action, improvise clever solutions, or create a complication that requires recovery.

### Cammy Clammy

A very cautious, worrisome clam.

- **Comic engine:** She examines every possibility, including wildly unlikely ones.
- **Catchphrase:** “Let me overthink this...”
- **Potential story value:** Her worry can slow the group down, but careful attention may also reveal a genuine risk, contradiction, or overlooked clue.

Cammy should not be written as merely frightened or incapable. Her caution should sometimes be useful.

### Frank

A small, adorable boss otter who is bossy and very mean. The concept is inspired by the Muppet otter Joe from Legal.

- **Favorite food:** Clam spaghetti.
- **Relationship:** Cammy is scared of him.
- **Catchphrase:** “N.O.M.B.” — “None of my business.”
- **Comic engine:** His cute appearance conflicts with his intimidating, dismissive behavior.
- **Potential story value:** Can obstruct help, impose ridiculous rules, or reluctantly reveal useful information.

Use the clam-spaghetti joke and Cammy's fear as exaggerated comic tension. Do not let the relationship become cruel or deprive Cammy of agency.

### Steve

A farting pufferfish.

- **Comic engine:** When startled, he puffs up and then deflates by farting out the air.
- **Potential story value:** His involuntary reaction can interrupt a tense moment, expose a hiding place, move an object, or trigger a funny complication.

Use the gag selectively so it remains surprising and does not overwhelm the mystery or reasoning task.

## Case 02 approved visual anchor

The approved reference is `../assets/style-anchors/Case02_Style_Anchor_001.jpg`.

Case 02 visual decisions:

- **Remy:** Preserve his established identity while using clean fur color shapes and only a few deliberate texture marks rather than dense fur strokes.
- **Sunburned Calamari:** Preserve the pointed squid-shaped head and coral/tanned coloring. Use casual sunglasses, a simple-pattern Hawaiian shirt, plain shorts, and flip-flops. His body has exactly two human-like arms and hands and two human-like legs; do not add tentacles or extra appendages.
- **Cammy Clammy:** Preserve her clam-shell head, glasses, and cautious expression. Use a low-detail professional coat or jacket with broad flat color areas and no plaid or woven texture.
- **DASH-3:** Preserve the approved compact delivery-robot design as shown.

These are approved Case 02 production references. Future cases may reuse them when appropriate, but should still apply story-specific casting and observation rather than treating every detail as an untested universal standard.
