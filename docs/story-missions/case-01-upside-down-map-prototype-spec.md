# Case 01 Prototype Spec - The Mystery of the Upside-Down Map

## Status

Prototype for the first story-integrated Thinking Missions case.

The production and rendered-page QA record for Version 002 is documented in `../production-notes/Case1_UpsideDownMap_002-QA.md`.

The story is a short mystery/adventure featuring Remy the Raccoon. Five Thinking Missions are embedded at natural suspense points. Completing each mission unlocks the next story development.

## Case goal

Test whether a self-contained five-mission mystery can increase motivation to continue while preserving the project's reasoning-first philosophy.

## Story summary

Remy receives a mysterious package containing an old map and a note warning him not to trust a duck. The map appears wrong: landmarks are upside down or on the wrong side. As Remy follows a sequence of clues, he visits a broken town clock, follows several woodland paths, discovers an abandoned shed, wrongly suspects Dottie Duck, and finally realizes that the duck has been trying to help him. The corrected map leads to the hidden Old Thinking Detectives' Hideout. Inside, Remy finds the badge of the first Thinking Detective and a message about what to do when you do not yet know what to do.

A final comic button reveals Remy's missing purple slipper inside the hideout, leaving the duck's involvement playfully unresolved.

## Story rhythm

- Opening: suspicious package and upside-down map
- Mission 1
- Reveal: hidden text points to the broken clock
- Mission 2
- Reveal: clue points to where three paths become one
- Mission 3
- Reveal: path leads to the suspicious shed
- Mission 4
- Reveal: Dottie Duck is not the culprit; she has been helping
- Mission 5
- Finale: hidden Thinking Detectives' room and original badge
- Badge tally / reflection

## Mission classification

| Mission | Primary Thinking Dimension | Secondary Dimensions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 - Which Way Is Up? | Representation & Model Building | Useful-Fact & Anchor Finding; Checking & Error Detection |
| 2 - The Clock That Stopped | Working Backward | Useful-Fact & Anchor Finding; Decomposition |
| 3 - The Three-Part Trail | Decomposition | Useful-Fact & Anchor Finding; Representation & Model Building |
| 4 - The Suspicious Shed | Elimination & Constraint Reasoning | Relationship & Pattern Reasoning; Checking & Error Detection |
| 5 - Beat the Sunset | Sequencing & Planning | Elimination & Constraint Reasoning; Checking & Error Detection |

## Mission 1 - Which Way Is Up?

### Story situation

Remy stands on the clubhouse porch. He knows:

- the clock tower is straight ahead;
- the pond is on his left;
- the windmill is behind him.

On the map:

- the clock tower is at the bottom;
- the pond is on the right;
- the windmill is at the top.

### Task

Something is wrong with Remy's map. He does not want to redraw anything.

**What could he do to the map so that what he sees on the map matches what he sees around him? Show or explain how you figured it out.**

### Intended insight

Turn the map halfway around - 180 degrees.

The mission should allow physical manipulation of the printed page or a detachable/rotatable map card if practical.

### Hint ladder

1. Pick just one landmark. Where should the clock tower be if it is straight ahead?
2. Instead of moving the landmarks, could you move the paper?
3. Try turning the map around. Check the pond and windmill too.

### Story unlock

Tiny writing becomes readable:

**FIRST CLUE: WHERE TIME STANDS STILL.**

## Mission 2 - The Clock That Stopped

### Story situation

At the old clock, a plaque says:

- the clock stopped at 4:10;
- the first Thinking Detective hid the blue envelope 35 minutes before the clock stopped.

### Task

**What time was the envelope hidden? Show your path before writing your answer.**

### Intended answer

3:35.

### Possible reasoning paths

- 4:10 -> 4:00 uses 10 minutes; go back 25 more minutes to 3:35.
- 4:10 -> 3:40 uses 30 minutes; go back 5 more minutes to 3:35.

### Hint ladder

1. Are you moving forward in time or backward?
2. Is there an easy time close to 4:10 that could be a useful anchor?
3. Go backward from 4:10 to 4:00 first. How many of the 35 minutes have you used?
4. You used 10 minutes. There are 25 more minutes to travel backward.

### Story unlock

A hidden clock compartment opens. The blue envelope says:

**The next clue waits where THREE SMALL PATHS BECOME ONE.**

## Mission 3 - The Three-Part Trail

### Story situation

At the giant oak, Remy learns that the hidden shed is 12 trail markers away.

Known segments:

- Oak -> Nose Rock = 3 markers
- Nose Rock -> Y-Tree = 4 markers
- Y-Tree -> Squeaky Bridge = 2 markers

### Task

**How many more trail markers are there from the Squeaky Bridge to the hidden shed? Build or draw the trail if that helps.**

### Intended answer

3 more trail markers.

### Possible reasoning paths

- 3 + 4 + 2 = 9; 12 - 9 = 3.
- Reach 9 at the bridge, then count 10, 11, 12: 3 more.

### Hint ladder

1. Which parts of the 12-marker journey do you already know?
2. Try drawing the whole trail as connected pieces.
3. How many markers have been accounted for by the time Remy reaches the bridge?
4. 3 + 4 + 2 = 9. The entire trail is 12.

### Story unlock

Three more trail markers lead to the abandoned shed. Someone has already been there.

## Mission 4 - The Suspicious Shed

### Story situation

Three objects are inside the shed:

- a red feather;
- a muddy boot print;
- half of a peanut-butter sandwich.

Three animals visited earlier:

- Professor Pigeon;
- Benny Beaver;
- Dottie Duck.

Each left exactly one object.

Facts:

- Professor Pigeon left the red feather.
- Benny Beaver hates peanut butter, so the sandwich was not his.
- Dottie Duck does not wear boots.
- The animal who left the boot print was carrying the missing map.

### Task

**Who was carrying the missing map? Do not guess: start from what you know for sure and cross out what cannot be true.**

### Intended answer

Benny Beaver.

Assignments:

- Professor Pigeon -> feather
- Benny Beaver -> boot print
- Dottie Duck -> sandwich

### Hint ladder

1. Start with the strongest fact. Who definitely left the feather?
2. Once Professor Pigeon has the feather, only the sandwich and boot print remain.
3. Benny cannot have the sandwich. Which object must therefore belong to Benny?

### Checking prompt

Does every animal have exactly one object? Did any clue get broken?

### Story unlock

Remy realizes Dottie Duck did not steal the map. She has been trying to help. Dottie pecks the map and helps Remy notice how to orient it correctly.

## Mission 5 - Beat the Sunset

### Story situation

It is 5:20 p.m. The hidden entrance closes at 6:00 p.m.

Before opening it, Remy needs:

- the secret code from the sundial;
- the brass key from the bridge.

He may collect them in either order but must have both before going to the oak door.

### Route information

#### Plan A - Key first

- Shed -> Bridge: 5 min
- Get key: 5 min
- Bridge -> Sundial: 8 min
- Read code: 5 min
- Sundial -> Oak: 7 min
- Open door: 5 min

#### Plan B - Code first

- Shed -> Sundial: 7 min
- Read code: 5 min
- Sundial -> Bridge: 8 min
- Get key: 5 min
- Bridge -> Oak: 6 min
- Open door: 5 min

### Task

**Remy has 40 minutes before the entrance closes. Can he make it? Build a plan and show your path. Bonus: is there more than one plan that works?**

### Intended solutions

Plan A totals 35 minutes and finishes at 5:55.

Plan B totals 36 minutes and finishes at 5:56.

Both plans work.

### Educational payoff

The mission intentionally demonstrates that more than one well-reasoned plan can be valid.

### Story unlock

The oak door opens and Remy finds the Old Thinking Detectives' Hideout.

## Finale message

Inside is the badge of Thinking Detective #1 and a note:

> A Thinking Detective isn't someone who always knows what to do.
>
> A Thinking Detective is someone who knows what to do when they DON'T know what to do.
>
> Find something you know. Try something. Check what happened. And keep thinking.

Dottie then finds Remy's missing purple slipper in the hideout, creating a final silly mystery.

## Badge tally

The case-ending page should focus on thinking behaviors rather than correctness. Suggested badge categories:

- Clue Finder
- Path Builder
- Brave Try
- Check-It
- Try Again

The learner can mark badges earned across all five missions and briefly reflect on which thinking move felt strongest or most useful.

## Prototype evaluation questions

Observe:

- Did the learner want to continue to learn the next story development?
- Were the story passages an appropriate length?
- Did the story encourage engagement or cause rushing through the reasoning?
- Which mission felt most/least naturally connected to the plot?
- Was the balance of five different Primary Thinking Dimensions useful?
- Did the badge tally add value after the stronger intrinsic reward of story progression?
- Was five missions too many, too few, or about right for one case?
