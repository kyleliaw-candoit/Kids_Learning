# Thinking Detectives Case 02 — Requirements

## Status

**Approved — requirements approved on 2026-09-10. Case 02 may proceed to canonical-material review and the text-first Character Lab; later approval gates remain in force.**

This document defines the boundaries for Case 02. It does not select a story, missions, setting, or cast.

## 1. Purpose

Case 02 should continue developing Amira's general problem-solving confidence and reasoning ability through a self-contained mystery or adventure.

It should improve on Case 01 based on her feedback without assuming that Case 02 must reproduce Case 01's structure, pacing, visual treatment, or mission count.

## 2. Learning requirements

Case 02 must:

- preserve the project's reasoning-first learning philosophy;
- keep **Show Your Path before Your Answer**;
- emphasize understanding, noticing useful facts, building a path, testing ideas, checking results, and revising when necessary;
- keep computation manageable enough that it does not crowd out reasoning;
- allow multiple valid solution paths whenever the task genuinely supports them;
- use progressive optional hints rather than immediately explaining the method;
- recognize productive thinking behaviors, not correctness or speed alone;
- never punish the learner by withholding the story because of an incorrect answer;
- include meaningful checking prompts;
- assign each mission exactly one Primary Thinking Dimension and no more than two Secondary Thinking Dimensions;
- keep educational architecture separate from—but coordinated with—the story architecture.

## 3. Audience and accessibility

Case 02 should:

- feel entertaining and visually appropriate for children around age ten and older;
- remain readable and solvable by Amira at age nine;
- avoid making vocabulary, sentence structure, computation, or instructions harder merely to appear more mature;
- use an older-kid tone through humor, characterization, pacing, and presentation;
- avoid preschool language, excessive cheerleading, and toddler-picture-book styling.

The intended target is:

> **Older-kid appeal without raising the accessibility floor.**

## 4. Story requirements

Case 02 must:

- be a self-contained mystery, adventure, or detective case;
- have a clear goal or question that creates genuine curiosity;
- give each mission a natural or understandable reason to exist;
- make every completed mission unlock a meaningful development, such as a clue, location, discovery, reversal, complication, or reveal;
- provide a satisfying finale and resolve the central case;
- use story segments that are substantial enough to create interest but short enough not to bury the reasoning work;
- use humor that comes from character personalities, choices, reactions, and consequences;
- avoid disconnected jokes that interrupt instructions or obscure clues;
- allow a short comic ending or future-case teaser only after the main mystery has been resolved.

Case 02 does not have to reproduce Case 01's five-mission rhythm. The mission count should be selected based on educational need, pacing, and expected learner stamina.

## 5. Remy requirements

Remy must remain:

- the main recurring Thinking Detectives character;
- a curious thinking sidekick rather than an all-knowing teacher;
- playful, goofy, expressive, and memorable;
- comfortable making reasonable mistakes or looking confused;
- encouraging of attempts, checking, persistence, and revision;
- consistent with his approved visual identity.

Before writing the Case 02 story, a text-first Character Lab should define:

- Remy's comic engine;
- what he tends to overdo;
- what excites, distracts, or flusters him;
- how he reacts when a theory fails;
- his recurring habits and verbal style;
- how his personality can support the learning philosophy;
- how he contrasts with possible supporting characters.

The Character Lab may strengthen Remy's characterization but must not turn him into an incompetent clown or an instructor who already knows every answer.

## 6. Supporting-character requirements

Sunburned Calamary, Cammy Clammy, Frank, and Steve are optional cast resources.

Case 02 must not:

- include all of them simply because they exist;
- select a character before identifying a useful story function;
- let a running joke overwhelm the mystery or reasoning activity;
- make Cammy helpless or treat Frank's behavior as sustained cruelty.

Supporting characters should be selected only when they contribute through:

- a useful personality contrast;
- a story role;
- a mystery complication;
- a clue or source of information;
- a meaningful reasoning situation;
- comic tension that improves pacing.

New characters may be created if the story requires a role the existing pool cannot fill.

## 7. Mission-structure requirements

Each mission must include:

- a clear story situation;
- a plainly stated thinking task;
- a deliberate reasoning target;
- an appropriate productive-struggle target;
- a progressive hint ladder;
- more than one valid solution path when appropriate;
- a checking prompt;
- a meaningful story unlock;
- relevant process-based badge opportunities;
- adequate space to show, draw, organize, calculate, or explain a path;
- a final-answer area that appears after the reasoning area.

The missions should vary their Primary Thinking Dimensions unless Case 02 is deliberately approved as focused practice.

The final educational plan must explain:

- why each mission was selected;
- how difficulty changes across the case;
- what should be challenging;
- what should remain easy;
- how each mission contributes to the overall capability mix;
- how the story connection supports rather than weakens the reasoning task.

## 8. Instructions and writing-space requirements

Learner-facing instructions must:

- make the required action immediately understandable;
- separate story prose from mission instructions;
- clearly indicate when the learner should stop reading and solve;
- clearly indicate where hints are located and that they are optional;
- avoid giving away the intended reasoning method prematurely;
- use readable type and uncomplicated page hierarchy.

Writing regions must:

- be designed for a nine-year-old's handwriting, not adult-sized handwriting;
- provide enough room for drawings, diagrams, crossed-out attempts, and revisions;
- prioritize working space over decorative artwork;
- keep **Show Your Path** visually before and more prominent than **Your Answer**.

## 9. Illustration requirements

Case 02 should use a **middle-grade illustrated-fiction/editorial aesthetic**.

Approved characteristics:

- medium-detail, hand-drawn characters and important objects;
- confident ink or pencil-style linework;
- expressive poses and facial reactions;
- clear silhouettes;
- selective texture and shading;
- purposeful negative space;
- visual energy without edge-to-edge decoration;
- grayscale, black-and-white, or restrained color;
- a small coordinated palette when color is used.

Avoid:

- toddler-picture-book treatment;
- uniformly bright or highly saturated scenes;
- extremely rounded, baby-like character presentation;
- glossy 3D rendering;
- dense painterly backgrounds;
- excessive props or high-frequency texture;
- giving every object equal visual emphasis;
- abstract or unexplained decorative icons.

Use this focal hierarchy:

| Priority | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Mission-critical clues and functional diagrams | Highest precision and clarity |
| Main characters and important actions | Medium detail and strong expression |
| Key objects and active supporting characters | Enough detail to communicate their roles |
| Setting cues | Simplified shapes, linework, or tonal/color blocks |
| Pure decoration | Normally omitted |

## 10. Background and setting requirements

Backgrounds must:

- remain quieter than characters, clues, and important objects;
- use fewer secondary props, textures, colors, and contrast levels;
- include a few distinctive cues when the location's identity or atmosphere matters;
- suggest rather than fully render incidental trees, flowers, buildings, furniture, or bystanders;
- avoid making a decorative object look like a clue;
- reduce visual noise and unnecessary image-generation complexity.

“Richer worlds” means more specific and engaging than placeholder clip art—not maximum environmental detail.

## 11. Functional-graphic requirements

Maps, clocks, routes, timelines, logic grids, tables, and diagrams must:

- be constructed for accuracy first;
- match every written fact and constraint;
- make problem-critical information prominent;
- use readable labels and sufficient contrast;
- avoid unexplained symbols;
- remain understandable without adult interpretation;
- integrate visually with the adventure without sacrificing precision;
- be validated independently against the approved mission specification.

Generated story artwork must not be the sole source for exact puzzle information when a constructed diagram is required.

## 12. Visual experimentation requirement

Because the middle-grade direction is new, Case 02 should include a small style-anchor approval before full illustration production.

The anchor should test:

- Remy in the stronger middle-grade treatment;
- expressive character acting;
- simplified background treatment;
- purposeful negative space;
- palette restraint;
- readability at expected print size.

This is an implementation test, not permission to generate the complete illustration set. Full production should proceed only after the educational/story plan and style anchor are approved.

## 13. Deliverable requirements

The final Case 02 package should:

- use one PDF containing learner pages and the grown-up guide;
- include any required hint pages or stations;
- include a complete grown-up guide with intended insights, solution paths, hints, checking methods, and thinking behaviors to recognize;
- maintain clear separation between learner-facing content and grown-up answers;
- use a filename and version number consistent with repository conventions;
- be added to the canonical repository only after passing production QA.

## 14. Production Quality Gate

Before Case 02 is presented as finished:

1. Generate the complete artifact.
2. Render every page at readable resolution.
3. Visually inspect every rendered page.
4. Validate every functional diagram against the written specification.
5. Check layout, clipping, collisions, type size, writing space, page order, age fit, palette restraint, background noise, and Remy consistency.
6. Correct every blocking defect.
7. Re-render corrected and affected neighboring pages.
8. Render and inspect the complete final sequence again.
9. Record the defects, corrections, page count, diagram validation, and final result.
10. Present only the version marked **QA passed**.

Work should proceed in this order:

1. Approve these Case 02 requirements.
2. Re-read the current canonical repository materials.
3. Conduct the text-first Character Lab.
4. Design the educational architecture.
5. Develop the story concept, structure, cast, and illustration plan.
6. Present the combined educational/story plan for approval.
7. Develop and approve the small visual style anchor.
8. Produce the complete Case 02 artifact.
9. Run the mandatory Production Quality Gate.
10. Present the QA-passed PDF.

## Open decisions

- Case 02 premise and setting;
- mission count;
- Primary and Secondary Thinking Dimensions;
- difficulty progression;
- story length and page count;
- supporting cast;
- exact Remy comic habits;
- grayscale versus limited color;
- case-specific palette;
- badge implementation;
- exact visual style anchor.

## Resolved tensions

### “Richer worlds” versus quieter backgrounds

Settings should feel specific and intentional through selective cues, not dense decoration.

### Early character development versus story-driven casting

The Character Lab will strengthen Remy and clarify the optional character pool. Case 02 character selection will occur only after useful story functions are identified.
