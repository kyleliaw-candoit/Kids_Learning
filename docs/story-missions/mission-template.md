# Story-Integrated Thinking Mission Template

Use this as a design checklist, not a rigid printable layout.

## Mission identity

- **Mission title:**
- **Story position:**
- **Primary Thinking Dimension:** exactly one
- **Secondary Thinking Dimensions:** zero to two

## Story situation

What is happening in the story immediately before the mission? Why does the character need to solve this problem?

## Thinking task

State the problem clearly in age-appropriate language.

The task should preserve the project principle:

> **Path before answer.**

Whenever useful, ask the learner to explain, draw, organize, test, or show the route before writing the final answer.

## Intended reasoning target

What mental move is the mission actually designed to practice?

Do not define this only as an arithmetic operation. Describe the reasoning capability: e.g. working backward, decomposition, elimination, planning, representation, checking, or finding an anchor.

## Productive-struggle target

What should be challenging here? What should remain easy enough that the learner's working memory can stay focused on reasoning?

## Hint ladder

Design progressive hints from lightest to strongest:

1. **Attention hint** — point toward something useful without giving the method.
2. **Anchor/structure hint** — suggest a useful fact, representation, or relationship.
3. **Directional hint** — make the route more explicit.
4. **Strong hint if needed** — provide enough structure to restore movement without simply handing over the answer.

Not every mission needs four hints.

## Valid solution paths

List more than one reasonable route whenever the problem genuinely supports them.

Alternate paths should be presented as additional ways to think, not as corrections of the learner's method.

## Checking prompt

How can the learner test whether the result or reasoning makes sense?

## Story unlock

What meaningful development becomes available after the mission?

Examples: clue, location, suspect, twist, reveal, funny complication, or step toward the finale.

## Badge opportunities

Which thinking behaviors might naturally earn recognition?

- Clue Finder
- Path Builder
- Brave Try
- Check-It
- Try Again

Do not award badges merely for speed or correctness.

## Illustration plan

Identify the role of each planned visual before production:

- **Story scene** — shows a meaningful place, action, or reveal;
- **Functional graphic** — accurately supports reasoning, such as a map, route, clock, grid, table, or timeline;
- **Character reaction** — communicates curiosity, confusion, effort, surprise, persistence, or pride.

Apply the visual principle:

> **Simple characters, richer worlds, clear puzzle signals.**

Remy and recurring characters should remain simple and consistent. Story environments and mission illustrations should be visually richer and age-appropriate for approximately nine-year-old learners. Functional graphics must be logically precise and easy to interpret.

## Production Quality Gate — REQUIRED BEFORE PRESENTATION

Every generated mission or case must follow `../Production-Quality-Gate.md` before it is shown as finished work.

Minimum workflow:

1. generate the complete artifact;
2. render **every page** to an image;
3. inspect every rendered page for overflow, collisions, clipping, readability, writing space, diagram accuracy, illustration clarity, and age fit;
4. fix all blocking defects;
5. re-render and re-inspect affected pages;
6. present only after the artifact is marked **QA passed**.

A generated PDF that has not been rendered and visually inspected is still a draft.

## Grown-up notes

Include:
- intended answer or insight;
- likely reasoning paths;
- how far to let productive struggle continue;
- which hint to offer first;
- specific thinking behaviors worth recognizing.
