## Case identity

- **Title:** *Thinking Detectives Case 02 — The Case of the Runaway Robot*
- **Setting:** The OddSpark Invention Expo, a slightly chaotic fair filled with unusual inventions
- **Central mystery:** Where did Calamari's delivery robot take the Golden Question Mark trophy, and why did it take the wrong object?
- **Mystery type:** A logical mix-up rather than a theft, villain plot, or random malfunction

## Case-level educational spine

> **Find what matters → decode a hidden scale → check an idea → revise a path → build and use a model**

The story layer must preserve the complete approved specifications in `case-02-educational-architecture.md`. Story fit may clarify or motivate a mission, but it must not weaken the intended reasoning target.

## Cast

| Character | Story function | Thinking function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Remy** | Lead detective and learner's partner | Forms useful but occasionally premature theories; models checking and revision |
| **Sunburned Calamari** | DASH-3's inventor and source of technical information | Bold starter who underestimates consequences |
| **Cammy Clammy** | Expo safety coordinator and keeper of organized records | Models careful constraint checking without becoming helpless or always correct |
| **DASH-3** | Delivery robot at the center of the mystery | Follows instructions consistently but literally |

Frank and Steve are deliberately reserved for future cases. Adding them would dilute the three-character dynamic without filling a necessary Case 02 role.

## Character behavior

### Remy

Use the approved **enthusiastic over-detective** characterization.

Remy should:

- notice genuine clues;
- construct plausible theories;
- become certain a little too early;
- invite the learner to test those theories;
- respond to disconfirming evidence with curiosity rather than embarrassment;
- model the transition from checking to revision.

His main story arc is not becoming less enthusiastic. It is using that enthusiasm through the complete reasoning loop.

### Sunburned Calamari

Calamari creates the inciting complication through confident under-planning, but he remains a capable inventor who supplies useful technical facts.

Use **“What could go wrong?”** once, immediately before the inciting incident. Do not repeat it mechanically.

Approved Case 02 visual design: pointed squid-shaped head; coral/tanned coloring; casual sunglasses; a low-detail Hawaiian shirt with a sparse, simple pattern; plain shorts; flip-flops; exactly two human-like arms and hands; exactly two human-like legs; and no additional tentacles or appendages.

### Cammy Clammy

Cammy's careful records and attention to risks contribute materially to the investigation. Her caution should sometimes be excessive and sometimes exactly what the team needs.

Use **“Let me overthink this…”** sparingly. Do not make Cammy helpless, permanently frightened, or the automatic source of every correct answer.

Approved Case 02 visual design: retain her expressive clam-shell head, glasses, and cautious body language; use a simple professional coat or jacket with broad flat color areas and no plaid, woven texture, dense crosshatching, or unnecessary garment detail.

### DASH-3

DASH-3 is consistent rather than chaotic. The robot follows a literal delivery rule and does not behave randomly whenever the plot needs a complication. His visual design in the approved style anchor was accepted without revision and should be preserved.

## Mystery logic

Before the demonstration, Calamari prepares a small parcel labeled **FINAL PRIZE** for DASH-3 to deliver to location **F-5**.

The expo's Golden Question Mark trophy also has a display label reading **FINAL PRIZE**. The trophy is placed directly in front of the demonstration parcel. DASH-3 scans the nearest matching label, picks up the trophy, and begins its programmed delivery.

DASH-3 is therefore:

- not malfunctioning randomly;
- not being remotely controlled;
- not stealing the trophy;
- correctly executing an ambiguous assignment.

Location F-5 is **Final Station**, a model-railway invention exhibit. DASH-3 waits there for someone to sign for the delivery.

Every necessary part of this explanation must be supported by visible records, labels, or mission results. The finale must not depend on an unsupported twist, hidden assumption, or illustration-only clue.

## Story and mission sequence

| Stage | Story development | Educational purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | Calamari says, “What could go wrong?” DASH-3 promptly departs with the trophy. | Establish curiosity and comic stakes |
| Mission 1 | Determine where the investigation should begin. | Find useful facts |
| Unlock 1 | Tracks lead toward North Concourse. | Give the investigation direction |
| Mission 2 | Interpret DASH-3's partially labeled tracking scale. | Decode a hidden interval |
| Unlock 2 | The signal places the robot at Prototype Plaza. | Establish the robot's next known location |
| Mission 3 | Test competing explanations for DASH-3's behavior. | Check claims and locate errors |
| Unlock 3 | Evidence shows DASH-3 is following a delivery program. | Reverse the apparent runaway-robot theory |
| Mission 4 | Revise an interception plan after a route closes. | Preserve useful work and alter only what failed |
| Unlock 4 | The control booth reveals `ITEM: FINAL PRIZE; DESTINATION: F-5`. | Reveal the label mix-up and destination code |
| Mission 5 | Reconstruct the expo's delivery-stop model. | Determine that F-5 is Final Station |

## Mission 1 — The First Useful Trail

- **Primary Thinking Dimension:** Useful-Fact & Anchor Finding
- **Secondary Thinking Dimension:** Representation & Model Building
- **Story situation:** DASH-3 and the trophy have left the demonstration area. Several observations are available, but only some help determine where to investigate first.
- **Thinking task:** Select the facts that establish the useful first trail and explain why they matter.

Useful facts should include:

- DASH-3 has distinctive double-chevron wheel treads;
- matching tracks lead toward North Concourse;
- the alternate service passage remained locked.

Interesting but unnecessary details may include the trophy ribbon's color, a balloon popping, or Calamari's unusual sunglasses.

- **Productive-struggle target:** Distinguishing information that is true or entertaining from information that answers the current question
- **Checking prompt:** “Could you still choose where to investigate using only the facts you selected?”
- **Writing interaction:** Fact marking or sorting, then a generous **Show Your Path** region, followed by the answer
- **Story unlock:** North Concourse
- **Badge opportunity:** Clue Finder

## Mission 2 — The Half-Labeled Tracker

- **Primary Thinking Dimension:** Scale & Interval Reasoning
- **Secondary Thinking Dimensions:** Useful-Fact & Anchor Finding; Decomposition
- **Story situation:** A tracker in North Concourse shows DASH-3's last position on a partially labeled distance scale.
- **Thinking task:** Determine the value of one interval, label the missing lines, and identify the location aligned with the signal.

The main functional scale must contain five equally spaced lines:

1. `0`
2. unlabeled
3. `50`
4. unlabeled
5. `100`

The signal aligns with the line between 50 and 100. The intended reasoning is:

- two equal intervals span 50;
- one interval represents 25;
- the signal represents 75 meters;
- Prototype Plaza is marked 75 meters from the stage.

The learner must be able to annotate the scale directly. A short transfer check should use a horizontal orientation and different easy values to test interval understanding rather than memorization of the sequence.

- **Productive-struggle target:** Recognizing that the numerical change is distributed across equal spaces, not counting the visible lines
- **Checking prompt:** “Starting at 50, do two equal jumps land exactly on 100?”
- **Response order:** **Show Your Path** → **One interval represents** → **The signal is at**
- **Story unlock:** Prototype Plaza
- **Badge opportunities:** Clue Finder; Check-It

## Mission 3 — Remy's Extremely Convincing Theory

- **Primary Thinking Dimension:** Checking & Error Detection
- **Secondary Thinking Dimensions:** Elimination & Constraint Reasoning; Relationship & Pattern Reasoning
- **Story situation:** The team finds DASH-3's diagnostic record at Prototype Plaza. Remy announces that someone must be controlling the robot remotely.
- **Thinking task:** Test three plausible explanations, identify the first fact that breaks each unsupported explanation, and repair Remy's theory.

The diagnostic record should show:

- power status: normal;
- remote connection: none;
- operating mode: delivery;
- route status: active.

The explanations to check are:

1. DASH-3 left to recharge.
2. Someone is steering DASH-3 remotely.
3. DASH-3 is completing a programmed delivery.

The learner should conclude that the first two claims conflict with the record and that the delivery explanation fits all available facts.

- **Productive-struggle target:** Resisting a plausible story long enough to test it systematically
- **Checking prompt:** “Does the repaired explanation fit every record, not only one of them?”
- **Writing interaction:** **Where does it first stop working?** → **How would you repair it?** → **Show Your Path** → **Your Answer**
- **Story unlock:** The investigation changes from searching for a controller or thief to finding a delivery destination.
- **Badge opportunity:** Check-It

## Mission 4 — Back Up One Paw

- **Primary Thinking Dimension:** Strategy Revision
- **Secondary Thinking Dimensions:** Checking & Error Detection; Decomposition
- **Story situation:** The team plans to reach the control booth, but a foamy invention demonstration closes the striped skybridge.
- **Initial plan:**
  1. Reach Blue Arch.
  2. Cross the striped skybridge.
  3. Enter the control booth.
  4. Send DASH-3's recall command.
- **Thinking task:** Identify what still works, locate the first broken step, and revise only the affected portion using an alternate connection through Workshop Hall.

The mission must not frame the entire first plan as a failure. Its central experience is preserving valid work while changing the obstructed middle.

- **Productive-struggle target:** Avoiding both blind persistence and unnecessary restarting
- **Checking prompt:** “Which original steps did you keep, and why do they still work?”
- **Writing interaction:** **What still works?** → **What will you change?** → **Your new path** → **Your Answer**
- **Story unlock:** The control booth displays `ITEM: FINAL PRIZE`, `DESTINATION: F-5`, and `STATUS: AWAITING DELIVERY`. Calamari recognizes that both his intended parcel and the trophy carried the same label.
- **Badge opportunities:** Try Again; Brave Try

## Mission 5 — Build the Delivery Model

- **Primary Thinking Dimension:** Representation & Model Building
- **Secondary Thinking Dimensions:** Sequencing & Planning; Elimination & Constraint Reasoning
- **Story situation:** The numbered labels have fallen off the F-directory. The team must reconstruct the delivery-stop order to identify F-5.
- **Thinking task:** Organize five location cards and five constraints into a model, then use the model to identify DASH-3's destination.

Location cards:

- Charging Dock
- Snack Lab
- Bubble Lab
- Gear Gallery
- Final Station

Constraints:

- Charging Dock is F-1.
- Gear Gallery comes immediately after Bubble Lab.
- Snack Lab comes before Bubble Lab.
- Final Station comes after Gear Gallery.
- Final Station is not beside Charging Dock.

The unique order is:

1. Charging Dock
2. Snack Lab
3. Bubble Lab
4. Gear Gallery
5. Final Station

The learner may use an ordered list, diagram, table, or movable-card arrangement. The page must not prescribe one representation so completely that the model-building work disappears.

- **Productive-struggle target:** Choosing how to organize several constraints while keeping computation light
- **Checking prompt:** “Can you use your model to verify every clue one at a time?”
- **Writing interaction:** The largest flexible working area in the case, followed by the answer
- **Story unlock:** F-5 is Final Station.
- **Badge opportunities:** Path Builder; Check-It

## Finale

The team finds DASH-3 beside the miniature railway platform, holding the Golden Question Mark trophy and repeatedly requesting a delivery signature.

The finale confirms:

- DASH-3 was programmed to collect an item labeled **FINAL PRIZE**;
- the trophy's display label used the same words;
- the trophy blocked the robot's view of Calamari's intended parcel;
- DASH-3 scanned the nearest matching label and delivered that object to F-5.

Remy formally retires his “International Robot Trophy Syndicate” theory. Calamari concedes that two identical labels may have been one thing that could go wrong. Cammy produces an already-prepared form titled “Preventing This Exact Situation Next Time.”

The central mystery must feel completely resolved before any optional comic button or future-case teaser.

## Humor controls

- Humor comes from contrasting reasoning habits, consequences, and character reactions.
- Catchphrases and jokes pause during learner instructions.
- Remy may be wrong, partly right, or correct; he must not become an incompetent clown.
- Calamari's mistake comes from insufficient checking, not lack of intelligence.
- Cammy's caution must contribute meaningfully rather than exist only as a fear joke.
- DASH-3's behavior must remain consistent with its rules.
- Running jokes should not obscure clues or functional graphics.
