# Story-Mission Chapter Structure

## Default rhythm

A typical short case should alternate between story progress and reasoning:

**Story Opening → Mission 1 → Reveal → Mission 2 → Reveal → Mission 3 → Reveal → Mission 4 → Reveal → Mission 5 → Finale → Badge Tally / Reflection**

Five missions is the current prototype default. Four to six may be appropriate when story rhythm or learner fatigue suggests otherwise.

## Chapter requirements

Each chapter should normally have:

- a self-contained beginning;
- a clear mystery, adventure, or goal;
- a reason the learner wants to know what happens next;
- meaningful unlocks after each mission;
- a satisfying finale;
- optional continuity hooks, but no dependence on a long novel arc.

## Meaningful unlock rule

A mission should unlock something with narrative value, such as:

- a clue;
- a new location;
- a suspect;
- a discovery;
- a reversal;
- a funny complication;
- a new piece of information;
- a twist;
- the final step toward the chapter reveal.

Avoid cosmetic rewards such as "Great job—now do another problem." The child should want the story reward for its own sake.

## Story length

Story segments should be short enough that they do not overshadow the Thinking Missions, but rich enough to create curiosity and emotional investment.

Current prototype question: does the child feel eager to continue without rushing through the reasoning just to reach the next scene?

## Ending

The finale should provide a genuine payoff for completing the sequence of missions. A short comic button, unresolved silly detail, or teaser for another case may be added, but the central case should feel complete.
