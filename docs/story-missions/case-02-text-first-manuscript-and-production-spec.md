## Page 2 — Your Case File

### Text

# Your Case File

At the OddSpark Invention Expo, strange ideas are normal.

A toaster that butters bread? Normal.

Shoes that tie themselves together? Not useful—but normal.

A delivery robot racing away with the most important trophy at the fair?

That is a case.

You are Remy's thinking partner. During this investigation, you will:

- find useful clues;
- show how you are thinking;
- test ideas;
- change a plan when the facts change;
- solve the mystery.

You do not need to know the perfect path before you begin.

> **Find somewhere to start. Try a path. Check it. Revise if you need to.**

### Small instruction box

When you see **STOP & SOLVE**, pause the story and complete the mission.

Optional hints are on pages 23–27. Use one only when you need help moving again.

## Page 3 — Welcome to OddSpark

### Text

# Welcome to OddSpark

The OddSpark Invention Expo buzzed, clanked, flashed, and—near Booth 12—made a noise like a duck sneezing into a trumpet.

Remy the Raccoon adjusted his detective cap.

“Excellent,” he said. “Everything here looks suspicious.”

“Most of it is supposed to,” said Cammy Clammy. She held an expo safety clipboard thick enough to stop a rolling bowling ball. “I have inspected every booth twice.”

She glanced toward the demonstration stage.

“Some booths three times.”

Onstage, Sunburned Calamari stood beside a compact delivery robot. His sunglasses rested above his eyes. His Hawaiian shirt had a few large flowers and no concern whatsoever.

“Meet DASH-3!” Calamari announced. “He can scan a label, find the matching destination, and deliver almost anything.”

The robot gave a cheerful beep.

Beside it sat a small silver demonstration parcel labeled **FINAL PRIZE**.

On a nearby stand gleamed the expo's Golden Question Mark trophy. A display card rested below it.

Cammy raised one finger. “Let me overthink this. Did you check what happens when two objects have the same—”

Calamari grinned.

“What could go wrong?”

## Page 4 — The Incident

### Text

# BEEP. SCAN. GONE.

DASH-3's scanner flashed.

**BEEP.**

Two robot arms lifted the Golden Question Mark.

**WHIRRRR.**

DASH-3 rolled off the stage, turned past a curtain, and vanished into the crowd.

For one very quiet second, everyone stared.

Then Calamari said, “I can explain.”

He could not.

Remy snapped open his notebook.

“A runaway robot. A missing trophy. A crowded invention fair.” His eyes widened. “This has all the signs of an International Robot Trophy Syndicate.”

Cammy looked at him. “Is that a real thing?”

“It has an official name,” said Remy. “That is usually a strong start.”

They hurried behind the curtain. Several observations waited there—but not all of them would help.

# Grown-Up Guide

## Page 28 — How to Use This Case

### Purpose

This case develops general reasoning rather than teaching a single school procedure. The learner practices finding a foothold, interpreting equal intervals, checking a claim, revising a plan, and building a model.

### Facilitation principles

- Let Amira read and attempt each mission before offering a method.
- Ask what she notices and what she could try.
- Protect productive struggle, but do not let it become paralysis.
- Offer hints in order and stop as soon as thinking resumes.
- Continue the story after genuine engagement, even when the answer is incomplete or incorrect.
- Recognize a specific thinking behavior rather than saying only “correct” or “smart.”
- Keep **Show Your Path** more important than answer speed.

### Useful prompts

- “What is the question actually asking?”
- “Which fact gives you somewhere to start?”
- “What could you draw or organize?”
- “How could you check that?”
- “Which part still works?”

## Page 29 — Mission 1 Guide

### Intended answer

Investigate **North Concourse**.

### Necessary and supporting facts

- A identifies DASH-3's tread.
- B matches the tread and provides a direction.
- C rules out the alternate Service Passage and strengthens the conclusion.
- D, E, and F are true or plausible but do not answer the immediate question.

A and B are enough for a reasonable first trail. Selecting C as additional checking evidence is also valid.

### Valid paths

- Match A to B directly.
- Group facts by subject, then select the wheel/track group.
- Cross out details that do not change the destination.
- Use C first to eliminate the Service Passage, then confirm with A and B.

### Behaviors to recognize

- explains why a fact is useful;
- avoids treating every fact as equally important;
- uses an eliminating fact as a check;
- changes a selection after rereading the question.

### Watch for

Does the learner select colorful or funny facts merely because they are memorable? If so, ask what the question needs rather than saying which cards are irrelevant.

## Page 30 — Mission 2 Guide: Main Task

### Intended answers

- One interval represents **25 meters**.
- DASH-3's signal is at **75 meters**.
- The location is **Prototype Plaza**.

### Intended reasoning

From 0 to 50 there are two equal intervals, not three. Therefore:

`50 ÷ 2 = 25`

The complete scale is:

`0, 25, 50, 75, 100`

### Valid paths

- count two equal jumps from 0 to 50;
- recognize the missing line as halfway between 0 and 50;
- split 50 into two equal parts;
- work backward from 100;
- label every mark and verify equal change.

### Critical observation

The central target is recognizing equal **spaces or intervals**. If the learner counts the three visible lines from 0 through 50 and divides by three, ask her to point to each jump physically.

Do not begin with “divide by two.” That would replace the intended reasoning with a supplied procedure.

## Page 31 — Mission 2 Guide: Transfer and Observation

### Transfer-check answer

The middle tick is **20**.

The ticks are horizontal, begin at 10 rather than 0, and use a different increment. This checks whether the learner understands equal intervals rather than remembering the main scale labels.

### Observe separately

If the learner struggles, note which step caused difficulty:

- choosing 0 and 50 as anchors;
- counting intervals instead of lines;
- splitting the difference into equal amounts;
- transferring the interval value to the next mark;
- reading the aligned signal;
- matching 75 meters to Prototype Plaza.

### Thinking behaviors to recognize

- labels intermediate marks;
- uses anchors;
- checks the unit across the entire scale;
- corrects line-counting after noticing the spaces;
- transfers the idea to a new orientation.

Do not decide from one attempt whether Scale & Interval Reasoning should become a permanent standalone dimension. Record the observed reasoning sequence first.

## Page 32 — Mission 3 Guide

### Intended conclusion

Explanation C—DASH-3 is completing a programmed delivery—fits all four records.

### Checks

- **A: Recharge** conflicts with `POWER: NORMAL`.
- **B: Remote control** conflicts with `REMOTE CONNECTION: NONE`.
- **C: Programmed delivery** agrees with `OPERATING MODE: DELIVERY` and `ROUTE STATUS: ACTIVE`, while contradicting no other record.

### Valid paths

- test the explanations in order;
- begin with the most specific system record;
- make a supports/conflicts table;
- eliminate A and B, then verify C separately.

### Behaviors to recognize

- checks an exciting theory rather than accepting it;
- identifies the first conflicting fact;
- distinguishes “not disproved yet” from “supported”;
- repairs the theory in response to evidence.

### Facilitation caution

Do not treat Remy as foolish. His remote-control theory is plausible before the diagnostic record is checked. The educational point is that plausible ideas still need evidence.

## Page 35 — Finale, Badges, and Story Facilitation

### Mystery explanation

DASH-3 was functioning correctly but responding to ambiguous labeling:

1. The instruction selected an item labeled **FINAL PRIZE**.
2. Both the intended parcel and the trophy matched that description.
3. The trophy was closer to the scanner.
4. DASH-3 collected the trophy.
5. The route model identifies F-5 as Final Station.
6. DASH-3 waited there for the required signature.

All necessary evidence appears before the final explanation.

### Badges

Badges recognize observed behaviors, not correct-answer totals. Useful examples:

- “You found the facts that answered the actual question.”
- “You checked every interval, not only one.”
- “You found the record Remy's theory forgot.”

## Functional graphic specifications

### FG-1A — Evidence cards

- Six equal-size cards labeled A–F
- Text is the authoritative source
- No pictorial embellishment that makes a useful card visually more prominent than an irrelevant card
- Enough white margin to circle or cross out a card
- Reading order unambiguous

### FG-2A — Main tracking scale

- Vertical orientation
- Five horizontal reference lines at exactly equal center-to-center spacing
- Labels: 0 m on the bottom line, 50 m on the middle line, 100 m on the top line
- First and third intermediate lines unlabeled
- Signal marker centered exactly on the line representing 75 m
- Avoid three-dimensional perspective, irregular tick spacing, or decorative grid lines
- Learner must be able to write 25 and 75 beside the missing marks
- Validate geometry numerically before export

### FG-2B — Transfer scale

- Horizontal orientation
- Three tick marks at exactly equal spacing
- Labels: 10 on first tick, 30 on third tick
- Middle tick unlabeled and indicated by a plain arrow
- The answer is 20
- Keep visually secondary to the main task

### FG-3A — Diagnostic record

- Four rows: Power, Remote connection, Operating mode, Route status
- Values exactly: NORMAL, NONE, DELIVERY, ACTIVE
- High contrast and clear row association
- No fake buttons, meters, or warning icons that introduce new evidence

### FG-3B — Explanation check table

- Three rows corresponding to explanations A–C
- Writing column wide enough for at least one short sentence per row
- Do not pre-mark any explanation as correct or incorrect

### FG-4A — Revision route map

- Use only these connections:
  - Prototype Plaza ↔ Blue Arch
  - Blue Arch ↔ Striped Skybridge
  - Striped Skybridge ↔ Control Booth
  - Blue Arch ↔ Workshop Hall
  - Workshop Hall ↔ Ground Passage
  - Ground Passage ↔ Control Booth
- Mark Striped Skybridge closed with a readable barrier and words, not an unexplained symbol alone
- Open route must remain visually traceable
- Do not add a direct Blue Arch ↔ Control Booth line
- Place labels away from route lines

### FG-5A — Location cards and model area

- Five equal location cards with names only
- Optional light cut lines that do not resemble puzzle constraints
- Five routing rules printed separately and numbered
- Working page may show small `F-1` through `F-5` anchors along its lower edge
- Most of the page remains open for a learner-created representation
- Do not supply arrows or a completed table that reveals the intended chain

## Text and typography controls

- Use readable body text appropriate for a nine-year-old without preschool styling.
- Keep paragraphs short and dialogue easy to track.
- Use bold only for headings, functional labels, and a small number of clue-critical terms.
- Do not use all caps for long instructions.
- Reserve all caps for brief interface records, `STOP & SOLVE`, and a few comic machine messages.
- Keep mission names and classification tags visible but subordinate to the thinking task.
- Never shrink body text or writing areas to accommodate an illustration.

## Visual controls

- Use `Case02_Style_Anchor_001.jpg` as the Case 02 character and rendering reference.
- Preserve Calamari's sunglasses, simple Hawaiian shirt, shorts, flip-flops, two human-like arms and hands, two legs, and absence of tentacles.
