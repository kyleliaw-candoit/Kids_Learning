# Thinking Badge System

Badges are a **complementary** gamification layer. They are not the main reward and should not become a score for correctness.

The main chapter-level reward is story progression: completing a Thinking Mission unlocks the next meaningful part of the case.

## Purpose

Badges make invisible thinking behaviors visible. They help the learner notice that strong problem solving includes more than arriving at the right answer.

## Current badge set

### Clue Finder
**Behavior:** noticed a useful fact, relationship, constraint, or anchor.

### Path Builder
**Behavior:** constructed a sensible way to start or linked steps into a route.

### Brave Try
**Behavior:** tried an idea without needing certainty that it would work.

### Check-It
**Behavior:** tested whether an answer, assumption, or intermediate step made sense.

### Try Again
**Behavior:** learned from an unhelpful path, revised the approach, and kept going.

## Awarding principles

- Recognize the **specific thinking move**, not just completion.
- A wrong answer can still earn one or more badges.
- Do not take badges away.
- Do not require all badges in every mission.
- Avoid turning badges into a competitive point total.
- Let a grown-up briefly name the behavior when awarding recognition: e.g. “You changed your plan when the first one wasn’t helping—that’s Try Again thinking.”

## End-of-case tally

At the end of a chapter, the learner can look back across the missions and mark which badges appeared.

The message should be:

> **Look at all the ways you thought during this case.**

The tally is a reflection tool, not a grade.

## Future evolution

If the badge system proves motivating, later versions may add dimension-linked badges or visual collections. Do not add complexity until learner behavior shows that the current simple system is useful.
